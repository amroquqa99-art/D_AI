# Comparing alternatives

Define the decision in terms of the user's objective and constraints. Compare only viable alternatives on criteria that can change the choice: expected outcome, cost, reversibility, evidence strength, and material failure modes.

Apply the same evidentiary standard to favored and disfavored options. Do not invent objections to seem rigorous or assume familiar practice is suitable. Distinguish measured results from estimates and assumptions.

When a reversible small experiment can resolve material uncertainty cheaply, consider it within the authorized scope. Recommend an option with concise reasons and the uncertainty that could change the recommendation. Avoid numerical scoring that implies precision unsupported by the evidence.
